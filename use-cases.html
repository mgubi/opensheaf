OpenSheaf: use cases

How the specification adapts to the people and institutions that keep and select scholarly documents. It is non-normative: where it and the specification differ, the specification holds. Commands of the reference tools, opensheaf-py (osheaf), are given as illustrations; any tool following the specification will do.

Three roles recur:

  • authors make documents and their versions;

  • archives keep documents, every version, impartially: an author's own archive, an institution's repository, an independent storage site;

  • selections vet particular versions (revisions) of documents: a journal, a digest, a review journal, a reading list, proceedings.

They map to the two kinds of collections of section 10: an archive collection pins objects whole and may follow them as they grow; a selection pins revision sets and changes only by an act of its curator. Copies are of two kinds too: a mirror copies a whole object (an archive keeping another archive's objects), an accepted copy takes one vetted revision into the selection's own archive.

Authors

A researcher's own archive

A mathematician keeps an archive of her papers on her website (the first archive, mgubi.org/papers, is one).

  • Each paper is a document object; each version of the paper, a document version, numbered by the archive from 1 without gaps. Versions imported from arXiv keep arXiv's label (v3) in external.

  • A draft or a submitted manuscript found later is deposited in its place in the history (history), without renumbering what exists; versions never public at the time carry a Created date instead of Available.

  • An erratum is a notice about a version; a retraction is a withdrawal. Both are description-only versions: nothing deposited changes.

  • The archive's main collection is an archive collection of her own documents, following them: the index shows the current version of each.

  • DOIs come from the Zenodo binding (appendix A), one per document version. 

# deposit a new paper: its LaTeX source and the PDF made from it
osheaf deposit kpz-uniqueness paper.tex \
  --pdf paper.pdf \
  --title "Uniqueness of the KPZ fixed point" \
  --author "Doe, Jane|University of Example" \
  --rights cc-by-4.0 \
  --stage AO
# import the new arXiv versions of papers already in the archive
osheaf arxiv import downloads --update
# an erratum about version 2, without changing version 2
osheaf notice kpz-uniqueness \
  --about 2 \
  --text "Lemma 3 is wrong as stated; corrected in version 3."
# update the main collection and the website, then check everything
osheaf collect
osheaf index
osheaf verify

Co-authors in different archives

Two co-authors each keep an archive; their joint paper is deposited once, in one of them. The other lists it in a collection of her own archive (for instance "papers with my students") as a member of another archive. In an archive collection with follow, her list keeps pointing to the current version as her co-author adds versions; nothing is copied, and no permission is needed. If she wants the paper in her archive too, and every version is openly licensed, she can mirror it: a byte-for-byte copy of the whole object, which stays the co-author's object (mirrorOf).

A research group, a seminar, lecture notes

A group keeps one archive for its members' papers, theses and slides, or each member keeps her own and the group keeps an archive collection of members of those archives. Lecture notes and slides are documents like papers (with types such as Text or Audiovisual); notes revised every year are versions of one document, or one document per edition when each edition should stay citable on its own.

Archives

An institutional repository

A university keeps an archive of its members' work.

  • Members may deposit directly in the institution's archive, whose main collection is an archive collection of its documents, perhaps with one archive collection per department.

  • Or members keep their own archives, and the institution mirrors their documents: each mirror is a whole copy, every version, kept up to date by copying new versions, and listed with mirrorOf in the institution's discovery document. Mirroring needs the right to redistribute every version (an open licence, or the authors' agreement); otherwise the institution only references the documents.

  • The institution is impartial: it keeps what its members deposit. It does not vet versions; that is what selections do.

An independent storage site

A preservation service (a library, a consortium, a site like Software Heritage) keeps copies of archives so that they survive their owners' servers.

  • It mirrors whole archives, or chosen objects of them, byte for byte; an archive mirrored entirely lists the copy in the mirrors of its discovery document, so that clients find it when the original fails (section 6.4).

  • Since every collection pins its members by digest, any copy whose bytes match is as good as the original: integrity comes from the pin, not from the place. Verifiers check mirrors exactly as originals.

  • Zenodo deposits (appendix A) and timestamps (section 13.2) are further witnesses that a version existed, with these bytes, at a date. 

# copy an object of another archive, byte for byte, with all its versions
osheaf mirror https://example.org/papers/ kpz-uniqueness
# check the archive; members of other archives are checked too,
# on their mirrors when their own archive does not answer
osheaf verify --remote

An archive that moves

An author moves her archive to a new domain. Its archive identifier (a tag: URI) does not change; collections pinned it with the base URL of the time, and clients that cannot reach it there try the mirrors. Pins stay valid, because they fix bytes, not addresses.

Selections

An overlay journal

A journal whose papers are kept by their authors (on arXiv, or in their own archives) accepts, after review, a particular version of each paper.

  • An issue is a selection: each member pins the revision set the editors vetted, usually the accepted version.

  • The authors' later versions are not in the issue: they have not been reviewed. A correction accepted by the editors adds a revision to the set, as a new version of the issue, recorded in its inventory as the editors' act.

  • To make sure what it vetted stays available, the journal keeps an accepted copy of each vetted revision, as a document of its own archive: exactly the files of that version, byte for byte, with the source and its pin recorded. Only the copied revision needs a licence allowing redistribution; a paper whose first preprint was under a restrictive licence can be accepted in a later, openly licensed version. Later versions of the accepted copy (corrections, notices, a withdrawal) are the editors' acts; the authors' archive cannot change them. 

# keep an accepted copy of the version the editors vetted
osheaf accept https://authors.example.org/ network-discrimination \
  --version v2 \
  --stage VoR \
  --agreement "the journal's publication terms"
# build the issue, a selection, from the file listing the vetted
# revisions (issue-7.yaml, with "type: selection")
osheaf collection sync --from issue-7.yaml

A journal that publishes its own version

A journal typesets the accepted paper in its own layout, gives it a DOI, and publishes it under its licence, with the authors' agreement.

  • The journal's version of record is a document of the journal's archive, not a version of the authors' document (section 7.3): it has the journal as publisher, stage VoR, the journal's rightsList, the authors' agreement in object.json, and relations to the authors' versions (IsVersionOf, IsIdenticalTo the DOI it registered).

  • The authors' preprint is linked to it by a description-only version of their own document (IsVersionOf the article's DOI, section 8.1), if they want.

  • The journal's issues are selections of its own documents, or archive collections of them: the journal's own documents change only by its own acts in either case.

A digest, a reading list, a curated bibliography

A researcher publishes a list of recommended papers on a topic, from any archives.

  • The list is a selection: each member pins the revision she read and recommends. A later version of a paper enters the list only if she adds it, after reading it.

  • Nothing is copied, so no permission is needed; the members' cached citations keep the list readable if an archive disappears.

  • A selection may contain collections: a digest of digests, or of journal issues, each pinned in the version vetted.

A review journal (like Mathematical Reviews or zbMATH Open)

A review journal publishes short reviews of papers published elsewhere.

  • Each review is a document of the review journal's archive, written by a reviewer, with relatedIdentifiers Reviews pointing to the reviewed paper (its DOI, or the URL of the object version reviewed).

  • The reviewed papers form a selection: each member pins exactly the revision that was reviewed. When a paper gets a new version, the review stays attached to the reviewed revision, and the selection does not move; a new review of the new version adds a revision to the set, by the editors' act.

  • Reviews and the selection are kept by the review journal, so the record of what was reviewed, and in which version, is fixed and checkable even if the papers' archives change.

Proceedings, special issues, volumes

Proceedings of a conference are a selection of the talks' papers, each vetted in the version presented; a volume is a selection whose members are its issues (collections), each pinned in the version published. A special issue of a journal is a selection of the journal's own documents, or of accepted copies.

What happens when …

Event Archive collection (an archive, a mirror) Selection (a journal, a digest)
the authors deposit a new version it is followed (with follow): a new collection version pins the new head nothing changes; the curator may vet it and add it to the revision set
the authors add a notice or a description it is followed, like any new version not vetted; presentations may show it, saying where it comes from
the authors withdraw the document it is followed: the withdrawn document stays, with its notice the vetted revisions stay; the curator decides (a notice of its own, a withdrawal of its accepted copy)
the editors correct a vetted version not applicable a new revision in the set, or a new version of the accepted copy: the editors' act, in the selection's history
the member's archive moves the pin stays valid; clients try the mirrors the same
the member's archive disappears members are fetched from mirrors, Zenodo or snapshots; the pin decides the accepted copy keeps the vetted revision; references keep their cached citations
a version's licence forbids redistribution the object cannot be mirrored, only referenced the revision cannot be copied, only referenced (unless the authors agree)
